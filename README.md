## Executive Summary

The study investigates the key factors influencing users’ intention to adopt mobile banking services in Sri Lanka using the **Extended Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT2)** framework combined with **Trust constructs**.

Using **Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)** through **SmartPLS**, this research evaluates how performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and trust affect behavioral intention and actual adoption of mobile banking applications.

## Theoretical Framework

The research model integrates:

- **UTAUT2 Framework** (Venkatesh et al., 2012)
- **Trust Theory** for digital financial systems

### Key Variables

### Independent Variables

- Performance Expectancy (PE)

### Dependent Variables

- Behavioral Intention (BI)
- Mobile Banking Adoption

### Additional Relationship Tested

- Effort Expectancy → Performance Expectancy

## Statistical Validation

### Measurement Model Assessment

The outer model was validated using:

- Indicator Reliability (Outer Loadings > 0.70)
- Cronbach’s Alpha
- Composite Reliability (CR > 0.70)
- Average Variance Extracted (AVE > 0.50)
- Fornell-Larcker Criterion for Discriminant Validity

### Structural Model Assessment

Bootstrapping was used to test hypothesis significance.

Out of 10 hypotheses tested:

- 6 hypotheses were accepted
- 4 hypotheses were rejected

### Key Findings

| Relationship | Result |
|---|---|
| Behavioral Intention → Adoption | Supported |
| Effort Expectancy → Performance Expectancy | Supported |
| Facilitating Conditions → Adoption | Supported |
| Effort Expectancy → Behavioral Intention | Not Supported |
| Hedonic Motivation → Behavioral Intention | Not Supported |

This indicates that trust, facilitating conditions, and perceived usefulness play stronger roles than entertainment value or ease of use in mobile banking adoption.
